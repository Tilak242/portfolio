01

Deal models

Finance · M&A and LBO

Type
Financial models in Excel
Status
Case studies
Inputs
Public filings and deal announcements

Project brief

Two models of the kind an investment banking or private equity team builds: a merger model for a live strategic acquisition, and a leveraged buyout of a public retailer.

Paramount Skydance and Warner Bros. Discovery
  • Valued the deal at $110B enterprise value and $81B equity value, at $31.00 per share in cash.
  • Sources and uses: $47B of equity funding against WBD's existing debt of about $33B.
  • Accretion and dilution across financing and synergy scenarios, with $6B+ of cost synergies.

Approach

Every assumption is written down with its source, so a reviewer can trace each number. The merger model is built around the announced deal terms. The LBO takes its inputs from the company's 10-K and 10-Q.

Academy Sports + Outdoors take-private
  • 20% premium ($58.19 per share), 5.5x entry EV/EBITDA, $3.8B enterprise value.
  • Debt at 4.5x LTM EBITDA ($3.1B), a five-year operating model, mandatory amortisation and a 100% cash sweep.
  • At a 6.0x exit, the base case clears both hurdles (2.0x MOIC, 20% IRR). Recommendation: proceed to confirmatory diligence.

02

Climate risk and credit risk in Indian agribusiness

Research · Under journal review

Co-authored, under journal review. A panel of 197 firms (1,964 firm-years, 2015 to 2024), with plant locations matched to daily rainfall data. I did the econometric and machine learning modelling: a fixed-effects panel regression and a causal forest. Highly leveraged firms saw a 12.7 percentage point larger drop in net working capital during droughts.

03

Climate, carbon and agricultural markets

Research · Co-authored

Co-authored a study of tail-risk spillovers, using CAViaR value-at-risk series and a quantile VAR with generalised forecast-error variance decomposition, on 10 years of Bloomberg and MSCI index data. Downside connectedness peaked at 87.88% in crises, with climate indices transmitting risk and agricultural markets receiving it. My part was the modelling.

Project brief

A mobile-first platform connecting fans, artists, venues and promoters across booking, ticketing, production, contracts and payouts. I joined at the idea stage. It is now a working product, and the founders are raising with early commercial commitments in hand.

What I built
  • A ticketing and allocation engine with multi-party ticket pools, time-bound reservations and seller-link attribution.
  • Stripe Checkout and Connect for onboarding, payments, settlements and payouts, with a ledger recording the money.
  • A modular backend on PostgreSQL and Redis, with offline door-scan reconciliation for live events.

Approach

Where money moves, the numbers have to reconcile. Pricing is computed on the server, payment events arrive through webhooks, and every payout traces to a ledger entry. I was one of two developers.
